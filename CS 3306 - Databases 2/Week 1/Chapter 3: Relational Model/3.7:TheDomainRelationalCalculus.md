## 3.7 The Domain Relational Calculus (DRC)

The **Domain Relational Calculus (DRC)** is the second form of relational calculus. While the Tuple Relational Calculus (TRC) uses variables that represent entire rows, DRC uses **domain variables** that take on values from the domains of individual attributes.

* **Theoretical Basis:** DRC serves as the theoretical foundation for the **QBE (Query-by-Example)** language, much like relational algebra serves as the basis for SQL.

---

### 3.7.1 Formal Definition
An expression in DRC is written as:
$$\{ \langle x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n \rangle \mid P(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n) \}$$
* $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n$: Domain variables.
* $P$: A formula composed of atoms.

#### Atoms in DRC
1.  **Relation Atom:** $\langle x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n \rangle \in r$
    * $r$ is a relation with $n$ attributes.
    * $x_i$ can be a domain variable or a constant.
2.  **Variable Comparison:** $x \Theta y$
    * Comparison between two domain variables.
3.  **Constant Comparison:** $x \Theta c$
    * Comparison between a domain variable and a constant $c$.

#### Formula Construction Rules
* An atom is a formula.
* If $P_1$ and $P_2$ are formulae, then $\neg P_1$, $(P_1)$, $P_1 \vee P_2$, $P_1 \wedge P_2$, and $P_1 \Rightarrow P_2$ are also formulae.
* If $P_1(x)$ is a formula, then $\exists x (P_1(x))$ and $\forall x (P_1(x))$ are formulae.
* **Shorthand:** $\exists a, b, c (P(a, b, c))$ is equivalent to $\exists a (\exists b (\exists c (P(a, b, c))))$.

---

### 3.7.2 Example Queries
To understand DRC, we look at how it breaks a relation down into its constituent parts (domain variables).



#### 1. Selection and Projection
**Query:** Find the loan number, branch name, and amount for loans over \$1200.
$$\{ \langle l, b, a \rangle \mid \langle l, b, a \rangle \in \text{loan} \wedge a > 1200 \}$$

**Query:** Find only the loan numbers for loans over \$1200.
$$\{ \langle l \rangle \mid \exists b, a (\langle l, b, a \rangle \in \text{loan} \wedge a > 1200) \}$$
*In DRC, we must explicitly quantify the attributes we do not want to return ($b$ and $a$) but that are necessary to identify the tuple in the relation.*

#### 2. Join across Multiple Relations
**Query:** Find names of all customers who have a loan at the Perryridge branch and the loan amount.
$$\{ \langle c, a \rangle \mid \exists l (\langle c, l \rangle \in \text{borrower} \wedge \exists b (\langle l, b, a \rangle \in \text{loan} \wedge b = \text{"Perryridge"})) \}$$

#### 3. Universal Quantification (Universal Truths)
**Query:** Find names of all customers who have an account at **all** branches in Brooklyn.
$$\{ \langle c \rangle \mid \exists n (\langle c, n \rangle \in \text{customer}) \wedge \forall x, y, z (\langle x, y, z \rangle \in \text{branch} \wedge y = \text{"Brooklyn"} \Rightarrow \exists a, b (\langle a, x, b \rangle \in \text{account} \wedge \langle c, a \rangle \in \text{depositor})) \}$$

### 3.7.3 Safety of Expressions
Like TRC, DRC can produce infinite relations if not restricted (e.g., $\{ \langle l, b, a \rangle \mid \neg(\langle l, b, a \rangle \in \text{loan}) \}$).

An expression is **Safe** if it meets three criteria:
1.  All result values are from the formula's domain, $dom(P)$.
2.  For any **$\exists x (P_1(x))$**, the formula is true only if there is a value $x$ in $dom(P_1)$ that makes $P_1(x)$ true.
3.  For any **$\forall x (P_1(x))$**, the formula is true only if $P_1(x)$ is true for all values $x$ within $dom(P_1)$.

**Why this matters:** Without these rules, a database engine would have to test an infinite number of values for $x$ to see if a condition holds. Safety ensures we only have to check values that actually exist in the database or the query.

---

### 3.7.4 Comparison of Expressive Power
When restricted to safe expressions, the three main database languages are equivalent:
* **Basic Relational Algebra**
* **Safe Tuple Relational Calculus**
* **Safe Domain Relational Calculus**



| Feature | Tuple Relational Calculus (TRC) | Domain Relational Calculus (DRC) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Variable Level** | Tuple (The whole row) | Domain (Individual columns) |
| **Quantification** | $\exists s \in r$ (Bound to relation) | $\exists x$ (Bound to domain) |
| **Usage** | Basis for earlier formalisms | Basis for QBE |

**Note:** DRC does not natively include aggregate operations (like `SUM` or `AVG`) or arithmetic, but it is easily extended to support them in practical implementations.
