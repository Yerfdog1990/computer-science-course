# Section 22.1: Decision-Support Systems

## Overview

Database applications can be broadly classified into two categories:
- **Transaction processing** - Systems that handle day-to-day operational transactions
- **Decision support** - Systems that support business decision-making through data analysis

Transaction-processing systems are widely used today, and companies have accumulated vast amounts of information generated by these systems.

## Scale of Decision-Support Data

Company databases often contain enormous quantities of information about customers and transactions. The size of information storage required may range up to:
- Hundreds of gigabytes for medium-sized companies
- Terabytes for large retail chains

### Types of Data Stored

**Transaction Information** (for retailers):
- Customer name or identifier (e.g., credit-card number)
- Items purchased
- Price paid
- Dates of purchases

**Item Information**:
- Item name
- Manufacturer
- Model number
- Color
- Size

**Customer Information**:
- Credit history
- Annual income
- Residence
- Age
- Educational background

## Business Value of Decision-Support Data

Large databases serve as treasure troves of information for making business decisions such as:
- What items to stock
- What discounts to offer

### Real-World Examples

**Example 1: Retail Trend Detection**
- A retail company notices a sudden spurt in purchases of flannel shirts in the Pacific Northwest
- Company realizes this is a trend
- Company starts stocking larger numbers of such shirts in shops in that area

**Example 2: Targeted Marketing**
- A car company queries its database and finds that most small sports cars are bought by young women with annual incomes above $50,000
- Company targets marketing to attract more such women
- Company avoids wasting money trying to attract other categories of people

In both cases, the company identified patterns in customer behavior and used these patterns to make business decisions.

## Key Issues in Decision-Support Systems

The storage and retrieval of data for decision support raises several important issues:

### 1. SQL Limitations and Extensions

**Problem**: Although many decision support queries can be written in SQL, others either:
- Cannot be expressed in SQL
- Cannot be expressed easily in SQL

**Solution**: Several SQL extensions have been proposed to make data analysis easier

**OLAP (Online Analytical Processing)**:
- Deals with tools and techniques for data analysis
- Provides nearly instantaneous answers to queries requesting summarized data
- Works effectively even on extremely large databases
- Covered in detail in Section 22.2

### 2. Statistical Analysis

**Problem**: Database query languages are not suited to the performance of detailed statistical analyses of data

**Solution**: Specialized statistical analysis packages exist, such as:
- SAS
- S++

These packages have been interfaced with databases to:
- Allow large volumes of data to be stored in the database
- Enable efficient retrieval for analysis

**Note**: The field of statistical analysis is a large discipline on its own (see bibliographical notes for references)

### 3. Data Mining

**Definition**: Knowledge-discovery techniques that attempt to automatically discover statistical rules and patterns from data

**Data Mining Field**:
- Combines knowledge-discovery techniques from:
  - Artificial intelligence researchers
  - Statistical analysts
- Incorporates efficient implementation techniques
- Enables use on extremely large databases

**Coverage**: Data mining is discussed in detail in Section 22.3

### 4. Data Warehouses

**Problem**: Large companies have diverse sources of data that they need to use for making business decisions
- Sources may store data under different schemas
- For performance reasons (and organizational control), data sources usually will not permit other parts of the company to retrieve data on demand

**Solution**: Data warehouses
- Gather data from multiple sources under a unified schema
- Store data at a single site
- Provide users with a single uniform interface to data

**Coverage**: Issues in building and maintaining a data warehouse are studied in Section 22.4

## Scope of Decision Support

The area of decision support can be broadly viewed as covering all the above areas:
- SQL extensions and OLAP
- Statistical analysis
- Data mining
- Data warehousing

**Note**: Some people use the term "decision support" in a narrower sense that excludes statistical analysis and data mining.

## Summary

Decision-support systems leverage the vast amounts of data accumulated by transaction-processing systems to enable:
- Pattern identification in customer behavior
- Data-driven business decision-making
- Strategic planning and targeted marketing

Key technologies that enable decision support include:
- Extended SQL capabilities and OLAP tools
- Statistical analysis packages
- Data mining algorithms
- Data warehouses for unified data access

These systems help organizations transform raw transactional data into actionable business intelligence.
