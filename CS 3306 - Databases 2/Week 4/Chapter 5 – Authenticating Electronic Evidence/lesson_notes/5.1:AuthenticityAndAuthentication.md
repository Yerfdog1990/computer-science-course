# Authenticity and Authentication

## Introduction
Authenticity is the quality of something that is what it purports to be. When referring to documentary materials, authenticity requires that an entity maintain both its identity and integrity throughout its lifecycle.

## Definition of Authenticity

### Core Concept
- **Identity**: The entity remains the same as when originally created
- **Integrity**: The entity has not been corrupted or tampered with
- **Time Factor**: Must maintain these qualities from creation to use as evidence

### Professional Definitions

#### Canadian Government Standards Board (CGSB 74.32-2017)
"Authenticity is the quality of an entity that it is what it purports to be and that is free from tampering or corruption"

#### Association of Records Managers and Administrators (BSR/ARMA 19-2019)
"Record authenticity is the sum of the qualities of a record that establish the origin, reliability, trustworthiness, and correctness of its content"

#### InterPARES Research Project
"Authenticity is the trustworthiness of a record as a record; i.e., the quality of a record that is what it purports to be and that is free from tampering or corruption"

## Authentication Definition

### InterPARES Definition
Authentication is "a declaration of a record's authenticity at a specific point in time by a juridical person entrusted with the authority to make such declaration"

### Authorized Personnel
- **Public Officers**: Government officials with legal authority
- **Notaries**: Legal professionals authorized to certify documents
- **Certification Authorities**: Entities authorized to issue digital certificates

## Historical Evolution of Authenticity Concepts

### Classical World (Ancient Rome)
- **Custody-Based Trust**: Authenticity linked to custodianship rather than object characteristics
- **Tabularium**: Roman state archive guarded by quaestores (financial officials)
- **Access Control**: Users only accessed copies made by public scribes
- **Media Limitations**: Records on wax tablets were fragile and easily changed
- **Trust Model**: Trust in custodians rather than documents themselves

### Exceptions to Custody Rule
- **Public Records**: Records displayed in public places
- **Bronze Inscriptions**: Laws written on durable bronze
- **Marble Records**: Rolls of arms inscribed on marble

### Medieval Times
- **Shift in Trust**: Moved from custodians to records themselves
- **Media Evolution**: Transition from wax to more durable materials (wood, papyrus, parchment, paper)
- **Document Characteristics**: Formal characteristics became fundamental
- **Status of Transmission**: Degree of perfection (draft → original → copy) crucial
- **Self-Authentication**: Records had to be creditworthy on their own

### Diplomatics Science (1681)
- **Founder**: Dom Jean Mabillon
- **Purpose**: Assess authenticity and authority of Merovingian diplomas
- **Case Study**: Benedictine land rights in France
- **Focus Areas**: 
  - Documentary form
  - Status of transmission (degree of perfection)
- **Original Definition**: First complete document capable of reaching intended effects

### Document Classification in Diplomatics

#### Original Document
Three essential qualities:
1. **Primitiveness**: First in sequence
2. **Completeness**: Contains all necessary elements
3. **Effectiveness**: Capable of producing legal consequences

#### Draft Document
- **Position**: Precedes the original
- **Purpose**: Correction and refinement
- **Legal Status**: Not intended to have consequences

#### Copy Document
- **Sources**: May copy draft, original, or another copy
- **Purpose**: Communication or security
- **Variations**: Multiple generations and types with different authority levels
- **Multiple Originals**: Possibility of multiple authentic originals

## Legal Foundation

### Evidence Law Development
- **European Foundation**: Diplomatics formed basis of evidence and proof law
- **Core Assumption**: More authority = more believable content
- **Original Preference**: Original documents preferred over other transmission statuses
- **Authenticum**: Special copy with same authority as original

## Digital Challenges

### Transferability Issues
- **Concept Transfer**: Authenticity concept transfers easily from analog to digital
- **Verification Problems**: Digital authentication remains problematic
- **Declaration Complexity**: Authentication declaration more difficult for digital entities

### Standards Development
- **Increasing Need**: Growing number of standards addressing digital authenticity
- **Scholarly Focus**: Academic research on ensuring verifiable digital authenticity
- **Legal Adaptation**: Courts developing skills for digital evidence rules

### Jurisdictional Variations
- **Different Approaches**: Various jurisdictions handle digital evidence differently
- **Judicial Expertise**: Some judges developed specialized digital evidence skills
- **Ongoing Evolution**: Legal frameworks continue to adapt to digital challenges

## Key Principles

### Trustworthiness Components
1. **Identity Verification**: Confirming the entity is what it claims to be
2. **Integrity Assurance**: Ensuring no corruption or tampering
3. **Continuity**: Maintaining authenticity over time
4. **Verifiability**: Ability to prove authenticity when challenged

### Authentication Requirements
- **Authorized Declaration**: Must be made by authorized juridical person
- **Specific Point in Time**: Authentication valid at particular moment
- **Authority Basis**: Derived from legal or institutional authority
- **Documentation**: Process must be documented and traceable

## Contemporary Relevance

### Digital Preservation
- **Long-term Access**: Ensuring digital materials remain authentic over time
- **Technology Changes**: Adapting to evolving digital environments
- **Migration Challenges**: Maintaining authenticity through system changes

### Legal Proceedings
- **Evidence Requirements**: Digital evidence must meet authenticity standards
- **Court Acceptance**: Growing judicial familiarity with digital authentication
- **Standardization**: Development of consistent authentication practices

### Organizational Implementation
- **Record Management**: Integration into organizational recordkeeping systems
- **Compliance Requirements**: Meeting legal and regulatory standards
- **Risk Management**: Protecting against authenticity challenges

## Best Practices

### Documentation
- **Maintain Records**: Document authentication processes and decisions
- **Chain of Custody**: Track document history and handling
- **Authority Verification**: Confirm authenticator's credentials

### Technical Measures
- **Digital Signatures**: Use cryptographic methods for integrity
- **Hash Functions**: Create verifiable fingerprints of documents
- **Timestamping**: Establish temporal authenticity

### Procedural Controls
- **Access Controls**: Limit who can modify or authenticate documents
- **Audit Trails**: Maintain comprehensive logs of all activities
- **Regular Review**: Periodic verification of authenticity measures

## Summary

Authenticity and authentication concepts have evolved from ancient custody-based systems to sophisticated digital verification methods. While the core principles of identity and integrity remain constant, the methods for establishing and verifying authenticity have adapted to technological changes. Digital authentication presents unique challenges but benefits from established legal and diplomatic foundations.
