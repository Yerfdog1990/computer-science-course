# Selecting Backup Devices and Media

## Introduction

Many tools are available for backing up data, ranging from fast and expensive to slow but highly reliable. The right backup solution for your organization depends on several key factors that must be carefully evaluated.

## Key Selection Factors

### 1. Capacity
- **Definition**: The amount of data that needs to be backed up on a routine basis
- **Consideration**: Can the backup hardware support the required load given your time and resource constraints?

### 2. Reliability
- **Definition**: The reliability of the backup hardware and media
- **Consideration**: Can you afford to sacrifice reliability to meet budget or time needs?

### 3. Extensibility
- **Definition**: The extensibility of the backup solution
- **Consideration**: Will this solution meet your needs as the organization grows?

### 4. Speed
- **Definition**: The speed with which data can be backed up and recovered
- **Consideration**: Can you afford to sacrifice speed to reduce costs?

### 5. Cost
- **Definition**: The cost of the backup solution
- **Consideration**: Does it fit into your budget?

## Common Backup Solutions

### Tape Drives
- **Description**: Most common backup devices using magnetic tape cartridges
- **Capacity**: 100 MB to 2 GB
- **Speed**: Fairly slow compared to other solutions
- **Reliability**: Relatively inexpensive but not highly reliable
- **Issues**: Tapes can break, stretch, or lose information over time
- **Advantage**: Low cost

### Digital Audio Tape (DAT) Drives
- **Description**: Quickly replacing standard tape drives as preferred backup devices
- **Types**: 4 mm and 8 mm tapes
- **4 mm DAT Performance**: 
  - Speed: Over 30 MB per minute
  - Capacity: Up to 16 GB
- **8 mm DAT Performance**:
  - Speed: More than 10 MB per minute
  - Capacity: Up to 36 GB (with compression)
- **Cost**: More expensive than standard tape drives and tapes
- **Advantage**: More speed and capacity than standard tape drives

### Auto-Loader Tape Systems
- **Description**: Use a magazine of tapes to create extended backup volumes for enterprise needs
- **Operation**: Tapes within magazine are automatically changed during backup/recovery
- **Tape Type**: Most use DAT tapes
- **Magazine Size**: Typically 4 to 12 tapes
- **Drawback**: High cost
- **Advantage**: High-capacity solution for enterprise environments

### Magnetic Optical Drives
- **Description**: Combine magnetic tape technology with optical lasers
- **Media**: 3.5-inch and 5.25-inch disks (thicker than floppies)
- **Capacity**: 1 GB to 4 GB
- **Reliability**: More reliable than DAT
- **Advantage**: Enhanced reliability through hybrid technology

### Tape Jukeboxes
- **Description**: Similar to auto-loader systems but use magnetic optical disks
- **Operation**: Load and unload disks stored internally for backup/recovery
- **Advantage**: High-capacity solution
- **Drawback**: High cost

### Removable Disks
- **Examples**: Iomega Jaz drives
- **Usage**: Increasingly popular for backup
- **Advantages**: 
  - Good speed
  - Ease of use
  - Suitable for single drive or single system backup
- **Disadvantages**: 
  - More expensive than standard tape or DAT solutions
  - Disk drives and removable disks are costly

### Disk Drives
- **Speed**: Fastest way to back up and restore files
- **Performance**: Can accomplish in minutes what takes tape drives hours
- **Advantage**: Ideal when business needs mandate speedy recovery
- **Disadvantages**:
  - Relatively high costs
  - Less extensibility

## Buying and Using Tapes

### Tape Quantity Planning

The number of tapes needed depends on:
- Amount of data to be backed up
- Frequency of backups
- Duration for keeping additional data sets

### Tape Rotation Strategy

**Purpose**: 
- Increase tape longevity by reducing tape usage
- Reduce number of tapes needed while maintaining historic data availability

**Method**: Rotate through two or more sets of tapes on a scheduled basis

### 10-Tape Rotation Schedule

**Configuration**: 10 tapes divided into two sets of 5 (one for each weekday)

**Schedule Pattern**:
- **Fridays**: Full backups on Tape 5
- **Monday-Thursday**: Incremental backups on Tapes 1-4
- **Week Rotation**: Alternate between Tape Set 1 and Tape Set 2 weekly

**Weekly Schedule Table**:

| Day of Week | Tape Set 1 | Tape Set 2 |
|-------------|------------|------------|
| Friday      | Full backup on Tape 5 | Full backup on Tape 5 |
| Monday      | Incremental backup on Tape 1 | Incremental backup on Tape 1 |
| Tuesday     | Incremental backup on Tape 2 | Incremental backup on Tape 2 |
| Wednesday   | Incremental backup on Tape 3 | Incremental backup on Tape 3 |
| Thursday    | Incremental backup on Tape 4 | Incremental backup on Tape 4 |

### Off-Site Storage Enhancement

**Method**: Add a third set of tapes to rotate one tape set to off-site storage location weekly
**Benefit**: Ensures disaster recovery capability

### 24×7 Environment Adaptation

**Challenge**: 10-tape rotation designed for 9-to-5 work environments
**Solution**: Use 14-tape rotation with two sets of 7 tapes
**Schedule**:
- **Sundays**: Full backups
- **Monday-Saturday**: Incremental backups

> **Tip**: The 10-tape rotation schedule is designed for the 9 to 5 workers of the world. If you're in a 24 x 7 environment, you'll definitely want extra tapes for Saturday and Sunday. In this case, use a 14-tape rotation with two sets of 7 tapes. On Sundays, schedule full backups. On Mondays through Saturdays, schedule incremental backups.

## Conclusion

Selecting the right backup devices and media requires careful consideration of capacity, reliability, extensibility, speed, and cost factors. Understanding these elements and implementing proper tape rotation strategies ensures effective data protection and recovery capabilities for your organization.
