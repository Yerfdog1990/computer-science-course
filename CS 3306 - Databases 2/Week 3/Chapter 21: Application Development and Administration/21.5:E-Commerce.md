# 21.5 E-Commerce

E-commerce refers to the process of carrying out various activities related to commerce, through electronic means, primarily through the internet. The types of activities include:

• **Presale activities**, needed to inform the potential buyer about the product or service being sold.
• **The sale process**, which includes negotiations on price and quality of service, and other contractual matters.
• **The marketplace**: When there are multiple sellers and buyers for a product, a marketplace, such as a stock exchange, helps in negotiating the price to be paid for the product. Auctions are used when there is a single seller and multiple buyers, and reverse auctions are used when there is a single buyer and multiple sellers.
• **Payment for the sale**.
• **Activities related to delivery of the product or service**. Some products and services can be delivered over the internet; for others the internet is used only for providing shipping information and for tracking shipments of products.
• **Customer support and postsale service**.

Databases are used extensively to support these activities. For some of the activities the use of databases is straightforward, but there are interesting application development issues for the other activities.

## 21.5.1 E-Catalogs

Any e-commerce site provides users with a catalog of the products and services that the site supplies. The services provided by an e-catalog may vary considerably.

### Basic E-Catalog Requirements

**Minimum Functionality**:
- **Browsing Facilities**: Navigate through product categories
- **Search Facilities**: Find specific products quickly
- **Product Information**: Detailed product descriptions and specifications

### Advanced Browsing Features

**Hierarchical Organization**:
- Products organized into intuitive hierarchy
- Few clicks lead to desired products
- Category-based navigation
- Subcategory refinement

**Search Capabilities**:
- **Keyword Search**: Customer-provided keywords (e.g., "digital camera", "computer")
- **Advanced Search**: Multiple criteria filtering
- **Faceted Search**: Filter by attributes (price, brand, features)
- **Auto-complete**: Suggest products as user types

**Product Comparison**:
- Side-by-side comparison of competing products
- Feature comparison matrices
- Price comparison tools
- User reviews and ratings integration

### Catalog Customization

**Business-to-Business Customization**:
- **Corporate Discounts**: Special pricing for corporate customers
- **Volume Pricing**: Tiered pricing based on quantity
- **Contract Terms**: Custom terms for specific companies
- **Approval Workflows**: Multi-level approval for purchases

**Example Scenario**:
A retailer has an agreement with a large company to supply products at a discount. An employee of the company viewing the catalog should see prices as per the negotiated discount, instead of regular prices.

**Legal Compliance Customization**:
- **Age Restrictions**: Hide products not legally sellable to underage customers
- **Geographic Restrictions**: Block items not legal in certain states/countries
- **Regulatory Compliance**: Industry-specific restrictions (medical, financial products)
- **Tax Calculation**: Location-based tax display

### Personalization Features

**Individual User Personalization**:
- **Purchase History**: Recommendations based on past buying behavior
- **Browsing History**: Show recently viewed items
- **Preference Learning**: Adapt to user preferences over time
- **Special Offers**: Personalized discounts and promotions

**Benefits of Personalization**:
- Improved customer experience
- Increased conversion rates
- Higher customer loyalty
- Better cross-selling opportunities

### Database Requirements for E-Catalogs

**Data Storage Needs**:
- **Product Information**: Detailed product data, specifications, images
- **Customer Information**: User profiles, preferences, purchase history
- **Pricing Information**: Base prices, discounts, promotional pricing
- **Inventory Data**: Stock levels, availability, location information
- **Sales Restrictions**: Legal restrictions, geographic limitations

**Performance Considerations**:
- **High Transaction Rates**: Support many concurrent users
- **Fast Search Response**: Quick search results delivery
- **Real-time Updates**: Inventory and pricing updates
- **Scalability**: Handle growth in product catalog and user base

### Performance Optimization Techniques

**Caching Strategies**:
- **Query Result Caching**: Cache frequently accessed product data
- **Generated Web Pages**: Cache static catalog pages
- **Image Caching**: Optimize product image delivery
- **Session Caching**: Store user session data efficiently

**Database Optimization**:
- **Indexing Strategy**: Optimize search queries with appropriate indexes
- **Partitioning**: Distribute catalog data across multiple servers
- **Replication**: Create read replicas for catalog browsing
- **Connection Pooling**: Manage database connections efficiently

## 21.5.2 Marketplaces

When there are multiple sellers or multiple buyers (or both) for a product, a marketplace helps in negotiating the price to be paid for the product.

### Types of Marketplaces

#### Reverse Auctions

**Definition**:
- Buyer states requirements
- Multiple sellers bid for supplying the item
- Supplier quoting the lowest price wins

**Bidding Systems**:
- **Closed Bidding**: Bids not made public
- **Open Bidding**: Bids made public to all participants

**Use Cases**:
- Government procurement
- Corporate purchasing
- Service contracts
- Large-scale acquisitions

#### Auctions

**Definition**:
- Multiple buyers and single seller
- One instance of each item being sold
- Buyers bid for items
- Highest bidder gets item at bid price

**Multiple Item Auctions**:
**Complexity**: When there are multiple copies of an item, bid matching becomes complex

**Example Scenario**:
- Four items available
- Bidder A wants three copies for $10 each
- Bidder B wants two copies for $13 each
- Cannot satisfy both bids completely

**Resolution Strategies**:
- **Perishable Items**: Items of no value if unsold (e.g., airline seats)
  - Seller picks bids that maximize income
  - Time-sensitive decision making
- **Non-perishable Items**: More complex optimization required
  - Consider future value
  - Partial fulfillment possibilities

#### Exchanges

**Definition**:
- Multiple sellers and multiple buyers
- Similar to stock exchanges
- Market maker matches buy and sell bids

**Price Determination**:
- **Buyers**: Specify maximum price willing to pay
- **Sellers**: Specify minimum price wanted
- **Market Maker**: Decides on trade price (e.g., at sell bid price)

**Examples**:
- Stock exchanges
- Commodity exchanges
- Currency exchanges
- Real estate markets

### Database Issues in Marketplaces

#### Authentication and Security

**Bidder Authentication**:
- Verify bidder identity before allowing bids
- Prevent fraudulent bidding
- Maintain audit trails
- Compliance with regulations

**Secure Bid Recording**:
- Encrypt bid information
- Prevent bid tampering
- Maintain bid integrity
- Time-stamp all bids

#### Real-Time Communication

**Bid Broadcasting**:
- Communicate bids quickly to all participants
- Handle numerous buyers/sellers simultaneously
- Minimize communication delays
- Prevent information leakage

**Financial Impact of Delays**:
- **Market Volatility**: Price changes during delays
- **Auction End Games**: Last-minute bidding strategies
- **Trader Disadvantage**: Information asymmetry
- **Financial Losses**: Missed opportunities

#### High-Volume Transaction Processing

**Performance Requirements**:
- **Extreme Load Handling**: Stock market volatility periods
- **Auction End Times**: Concentrated bidding activity
- **Parallel Processing**: Handle multiple concurrent operations
- **Low Latency**: Millisecond response times

**Database Architecture**:
- **High Performance**: Optimized for speed
- **Large Parallelism**: Multiple simultaneous operations
- **Distributed Processing**: Load balancing across servers
- **Real-time Updates**: Immediate data consistency

### Technical Implementation Considerations

#### Scalability Requirements

**Load Patterns**:
- **Normal Load**: Steady-state transaction volume
- **Peak Load**: Auction endings, market events
- **Burst Load**: Sudden news-driven activity
- **Growth Planning**: Future capacity requirements

#### Data Consistency

**Transaction Isolation**:
- **Bid Conflicts**: Simultaneous bid handling
- **Price Updates**: Real-time price changes
- **Inventory Management**: Stock level consistency
- **Account Balances**: Fund availability checks

#### Fault Tolerance

**High Availability**:
- **Redundant Systems**: Backup servers and databases
- **Failover Mechanisms**: Automatic system recovery
- **Data Replication**: Real-time data synchronization
- **Disaster Recovery**: Business continuity planning

## 21.5.3 Order Settlement

After items have been selected (perhaps through an electronic catalog), and the price determined (perhaps by an electronic marketplace), the order has to be settled. Settlement involves payment for goods and the delivery of the goods.

### Payment Methods

#### Credit Card Payments

**Simple Method**:
- Send credit card number electronically
- Direct charge to customer account
- Immediate payment processing

**Security Problems**:

**1. Credit Card Fraud**:
- **Physical Goods**: Address verification prevents fraud
- **Digital Goods**: No address verification possible
- **Account Takeover**: Stolen credentials usage
- **Chargeback Fraud**: False claims of unauthorized use

**2. Seller Trust Issues**:
- **Overcharging**: Seller bills more than agreed amount
- **Data Leakage**: Seller passes card number to unauthorized parties
- **Misuse**: Card number used for other purchases
- **Privacy Concerns**: Unnecessary data collection

### Secure Payment Protocols

#### Protocol Requirements

**Security Features**:
- **Encryption**: All transmitted information encrypted
- **Authentication**: Verify all parties' identities
- **Privacy**: Minimize unnecessary information sharing
- **Integrity**: Prevent data tampering

**Encryption Technology**:
- **Public/Private Key Encryption**: Widely used for secure communication
- **SSL/TLS**: Secure socket layer for web communications
- **End-to-End Encryption**: Protect data throughout transmission

#### Man-in-the-Middle Attacks

**Attack Description**:
- Impersonate bank, credit-card company, seller, or buyer
- Steal secret information during transmission
- Intercept and modify communications
- Create fake public keys

#### Digital Certificates

**Certificate System**:
- **Public Key Signing**: Certification authority signs public keys
- **Chain of Trust**: Well-known root certificates
- **Authentication**: Verify identity through certificate chain
- **Certificate Validation**: Check certificate validity and revocation

**Certificate Hierarchy**:
```
Well-known Root Certificate
    ↓
Intermediate Certificate Authority
    ↓
End Entity Certificate (Merchant/Bank)
```

**Authentication Process**:
1. Start with well-known public key
2. Verify certificate chain in reverse sequence
3. Authenticate other keys through certificates
4. Establish trusted communication

### Secure Electronic Transaction (SET) Protocol

#### Protocol Overview

**Participants**:
- **Buyer**: Customer making purchase
- **Seller**: Merchant selling goods
- **Bank**: Financial institution processing payment
- **Certificate Authority**: Managing digital certificates

#### Communication Process

**Multiple Communication Rounds**:
- **Initialization**: Establish secure communication
- **Authentication**: Verify all parties' identities
- **Payment Authorization**: Process payment securely
- **Confirmation**: Confirm transaction completion

**Security Guarantees**:
- **Transaction Safety**: Prevent fraud and unauthorized access
- **Privacy Protection**: Limit information sharing
- **Data Integrity**: Prevent message tampering
- **Non-repudiation**: Prevent denying transactions

#### SET Benefits

**Enhanced Security**:
- **Multi-party Authentication**: All parties verified
- **Encrypted Communication**: All data encrypted
- **Certificate-based Trust**: Strong identity verification
- **Transaction Integrity**: Complete transaction protection

**Privacy Features**:
- **Limited Information Sharing**: Seller gets minimal buyer data
- **Bank Privacy**: Bank gets minimal purchase details
- **Data Minimization**: Only necessary data transmitted
- **Confidentiality**: Protect sensitive information

### Anonymous Payment Systems

#### DigiCash Payment System

**Anonymity Features**:
- **Untraceable Payments**: Cannot identify purchaser
- **Digital Cash**: Electronic equivalent of physical cash
- **Blind Signatures**: Privacy-preserving transactions
- **Unlinkable Transactions**: Cannot connect transactions to user

**Comparison with Credit Cards**:
- **Credit Cards**: Easy purchaser identification
- **SET**: Identifiable with bank cooperation
- **DigiCash**: Complete anonymity
- **Privacy Spectrum**: Range from identifiable to anonymous

#### Privacy Considerations

**User Privacy Needs**:
- **Personal Information**: Protect sensitive data
- **Purchase History**: Hide buying patterns
- **Identity Protection**: Prevent unauthorized tracking
- **Financial Privacy**: Protect financial information

**Regulatory Compliance**:
- **Anti-Money Laundering**: Balance privacy with regulations
- **Know Your Customer**: Identity verification requirements
- **Reporting Requirements**: Transaction monitoring obligations
- **Legal Framework**: Compliance with privacy laws

### Customer Support and Post-Sale Service

#### Support Systems

**Customer Service**:
- **Order Status**: Real-time order tracking

- **FAQ Systems**: Self-service support

**Post-Sale Services**:

**Customer Data Management**:
- **Order History**: Track all customer orders

- **Feedback Analysis**: Analyze customer satisfaction
- **Loyalty Programs**: Manage customer rewards

**Key Takeaways for E-Commerce**:

1. **Comprehensive Activities**: E-commerce encompasses presale, sale, payment, delivery, and support activities
2. **E-Catalog Sophistication**: Modern catalogs provide browsing, search, comparison, and personalization features
3. **Marketplace Complexity**: Different marketplace types (auctions, reverse auctions, exchanges) require specialized handling
4. **Security Critical**: Payment systems require robust security protocols and authentication mechanisms
5. **Database Centric**: All e-commerce activities rely heavily on database systems for data management
6. **Performance Requirements**: High transaction rates and real-time processing demand optimized database architectures
7. **Privacy Balance**: E-commerce must balance convenience with security and privacy requirements
8. **Integration Challenges**: Successful e-commerce requires integration with multiple external systems and services
