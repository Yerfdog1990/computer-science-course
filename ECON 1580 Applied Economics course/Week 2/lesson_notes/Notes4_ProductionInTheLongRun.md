# **Production in the Long Run**

## **Learning Objectives**

By the end of this section, you will be able to:

* Understand how **long-run production** differs from **short-run production**
* Explain why **all factors of production are variable in the long run**
* Interpret **short-run vs. long-run production functions**
* Understand what causes **diminishing marginal productivity**
* Analyze how firms adjust inputs when demand increases

---

# **1. Short Run vs. Long Run Production**

## **Short Run**

* At least **one input is fixed** (often **capital**, such as buildings or machinery).
* Firm can adjust **labor** easily but cannot quickly change the amount of capital.
* Production function in the short run:

>Q = f(L, K)
>where K = fixed capital.

## **Long Run**

* **All inputs are variable**, including **capital (K)**.
* Firms can adjust both labor and capital to achieve desired output.
* Production function:

> Q = f(L, K)

This flexibility allows firms to choose the **most efficient combination of labor and capital** for any level of output.

---

# **2. Short-Run Example: Typing Service with 1 PC**

Consider a secretarial firm that produces typing services.

* Current resources: **1 typist + 1 PC** → produces **5 documents/day**
* A customer requests **10 documents tomorrow**, so the firm wants to double output.

But in the **short run**, capital is fixed → **only labor can increase**.

### **Short-Run Production Table (K = 1 PC)**

| Typists (L) | Total Product (TP) | Marginal Product (MP) |
| ----------- | ------------------ | --------------------- |
| 1           | 5                  | 5                     |
| 2           | 7                  | 2                     |
| 3           | 8                  | 1                     |
| 4           | 8                  | 0                     |
| 5           | 8                  | 0                     |
| 6           | 8                  | 0                     |

**Interpretation:**

* Hiring the **2nd worker** helps: MP = 2
  → They can answer phones, assist with tasks.
* Hiring the **3rd worker** gives only 1 extra document.
* From the **4th worker onward**, MP = 0 → no additional output.

### **Why Do We See Diminishing Marginal Productivity?**

Diminishing marginal productivity (DMP) occurs **after the 2nd worker**.

Reason:

* Capital (the PC) is **fixed**.
* Multiple typists must share a single computer.
* Beyond 2 workers, additional labor cannot increase output because the **bottleneck is the fixed capital**.

---

# **3. Transition to the Long Run**

Suppose the firm now receives **regular demand for 15 documents/day** (triple the original amount).

In the **long run**, the firm can:

* Increase **labor**, and
* Increase **capital** by buying more PCs.

Let’s say the firm adds **two more PCs**, so it now has **3 PCs total**.

---

# **4. Long-Run Production: Expanding Capital to 3 PCs**

In the long run, the firm chooses L and K to maximize efficiency.

### **Long-Run Production Table (K = 3 PCs)**

| Typists (L) | Total Product (TP) | Marginal Product (MP) | Capital |
| ----------- | ------------------ | --------------------- | ------- |
| 1           | 5                  | 5                     | 3 PCs   |
| 2           | 10                 | 5                     |         |
| 3           | 15                 | 5                     |         |
| 4           | 17                 | 2                     |         |
| 5           | 18                 | 1                     |         |
| 6           | 18                 | 0                     |         |

### **Interpretation:**

* With 3 PCs, the first **three typists** each have their own workstation → MP remains high (5 per worker).
* Diminishing returns begin **only after the 3rd worker**, because that’s when workers must start sharing PCs.
* More capital shifts the production function **upward** and **delays diminishing marginal productivity**.

---

# **5. Comparing Short Run and Long Run**

### **Short Run**

* K fixed at 1 PC
* MP decreases rapidly after 1–2 workers
* Output capped at **8 documents**

### **Long Run**

* K can increase to 3 PCs
* MP constant for first 3 workers
* Total product rises up to **18 documents**

### **Graphically (described in words)**

Imagine two curves:

* The **short-run TP curve** rises quickly at first but **flattens** at 8 documents.
* The **long-run TP curve** sits **above** the short-run curve and continues rising to 18 documents.
* Each short-run curve corresponds to a specific K; the long-run curve is the **envelope** of all SR curves.

---

# **6. Key Insight: Efficiency in the Long Run**

Because **all inputs can vary**, firms in the long run can:

* Adjust labor and capital proportions
* Remove production bottlenecks
* Expand capacity to meet future demand
* Choose the **optimal input mix** for any output level

Thus, the **long-run production function** shows the **most efficient** way to produce each level of output.

---

# **7. Why This Matters for Firms**

Understanding long-run production allows firms to:

* Plan expansions
* Evaluate whether to buy more capital
* Decide on workforce size
* Forecast long-term average costs
* Achieve economies of scale (studied later in cost theory)

---

# **8. Summary**

* **Short run:** at least one factor (usually capital) is fixed → diminishing marginal productivity appears early.
* **Long run:** all factors are variable → firms can increase capital to maintain productivity.
* Adding capital **shifts the production function upward** and allows greater output.
* Long-run production shows the **most efficient** combination of inputs for any level of output.
