# **1. How Societies Organize Their Economies**

A modern economy is a vast and interconnected system involving production, consumption, and employment.
A key economic question is **how** all this activity is organized, coordinated, and regulated.

Societies have historically relied on **three primary economic systems**:

---

# **2. Types of Economic Systems**

## **2.1 Traditional Economies**

**Definition:**
An economic system where decisions are based on **custom, culture, and longstanding practices**.

**Characteristics:**

* Occupations remain within families
* Most people are **subsistence farmers**
* Production uses traditional methods
* What you produce = what you consume
* **Little or no economic growth or technological innovation**

**Examples:**
Rural regions of **Asia, Africa, South America**

---

## **2.2 Command Economies**

**Definition:**
An economy where the **government or ruling class** makes all economic decisions.

**Characteristics:**

* Government determines:

    * What goods/services are produced
    * How they are produced
    * Prices
    * Worker wages
* Government often provides key services (e.g., healthcare, education)
* Little individual freedom in economic choices

**Historical Examples:**

* Ancient Egypt (resources mobilized to build pyramids)
* Medieval manorial system
* 20th-century communist states

**Current Examples:**

* **Cuba**
* **North Korea**

---

## **2.3 Market Economies**

**Definition:**
An economy where **buyers and sellers** make decisions through voluntary exchanges in markets.

**Characteristics:**

* **Decentralized** decision-making
* Based on **private enterprise** (private ownership of resources and businesses)
* Prices determined by supply & demand
* Income depends on a person’s economic value to society

    * e.g., entertainers or athletes with high demand can earn more

**Famous Example:**

* New York Stock Exchange (symbol of market-based activity)

---

## **2.4 Mixed Economies (Most Real-World Economies)**

Most nations blend market and command principles.

**Examples:**

* **United States:** market-oriented with limited government regulation
* **Europe & Latin America:** more government involvement
* **China and Russia:** movement toward markets, but still closer to command-based systems

# **4. Regulations: The Rules of the Economic Game**

No economy is entirely free of regulation.

**Regulations enforce:**

* Contract laws
* Property rights
* Protection from violence/fraud
* Tax collection

Even command economies have marketplaces; they’re simply heavily regulated.

Over-regulation often leads to **underground/black markets** where transactions occur without government approval.

# **5. Globalization**

## **5.1 What Is Globalization?**

Globalization refers to the expanding **economic, cultural, and political connections** across nations.

Driven by:

* Advances in shipping (e.g., cargo/container ships)
* Telecommunications and computing
* Cross-border trade
* International agreements and economic partnerships

---

## **5.2 Measuring Globalization: Exports as % of GDP**

This measure reflects how much a country depends on selling goods/services abroad.

**Exports ÷ GDP = Share of production sold internationally**

### **Selected Export/GDP Ratios (2010–2015)**

| Country     | 2010 | 2015 |
| ----------- | ---- | ---- |
| **U.S.**    | 12.4 | 12.6 |
| **Belgium** | 76.2 | 84.4 |
| **Canada**  | 29.1 | 31.5 |
| **Mexico**  | 29.9 | 35.3 |
| **China**   | 29.4 | 22.4 |
| **Chad**    | 36.8 | 29.8 |

**Insights:**

* The U.S. has a **low exports/GDP ratio** because its large economy can meet most of its own needs internally.
* Smaller economies (e.g., Belgium, Korea) rely heavily on global trade.
* Many middle- and low-income nations (e.g., Mexico, China) experienced increases in globalization.

---

## **5.3 Resistance to Globalization**

Despite its benefits, globalization has sparked concerns regarding:

* Loss of local manufacturing jobs
* National sovereignty issues
* Economic inequality

Key events illustrating this pushback:

* **Brexit (2016)**
* **Election of Donald J. Trump (2016)**

---

# **6. Gross Domestic Product (GDP)**

**Definition:**
GDP is the **total market value** of all **final goods and services** produced within a country in a given period.

**What GDP Tells Us**

* Size of an economy
* Growth over time
* Standard of living (often approximated by GDP per capita)
* Economic comparisons across countries

GDP connects to globalization through international trade: **exports** add to GDP, while **imports** do not.

---

# **7. Bring It Home: Information Overload in the Information Age**

Today’s economic decisions—small and large—are driven by **data and information**, such as:

* Weather forecasts for farming
* GPS mapping
* Historical performance metrics
* Predictive analytics

Economic choices always involve **scarcity**, making good information essential for allocating resources effectively.

Information helps determine:

* What to produce
* How to produce
* How many resources to allocate
* What opportunity costs are worth accepting

This highlights the central role of **information** in modern economics.

---

# **Summary**

* **Traditional economies** rely on custom and show little growth.
* **Command economies** centralize decisions in government.
* **Market economies** decentralize decisions to buyers and sellers.
* Most countries operate **mixed economies**.
* **GDP** measures a nation’s total production and standard of living.
* **Globalization** has expanded global trade but also faced increasing resistance.
* **Information** is crucial for making better economic decisions in a world of scarcity.
