## 1. Introduction — Why Integration Becomes Necessary

### 1.1 Setting the Stage

> **After you have a sense of the different types of cloud environments and services that are available, you can think about how you can bring these clouds and services together to form a computing ecosystem that meets your needs.**

### 1.2 The Core Problem — Clouds Weren't Built to Work Together

> **Generally, clouds and services have been developed to provide their individual capabilities, but they may NOT have been designed to work together.**

### 1.3 Single-Cloud vs. Multi-Cloud — Where the Problem Actually Bites

- **If you use ONLY one cloud:** this **won't be much of a problem**, since **services within a single cloud platform have typically been developed together.**
- **BUT:** if — as **advised** — you're using **multiple clouds** in either a **hybrid** or **multicloud** configuration, then **INTEGRATION will become MORE of a challenge.**

```
Single Cloud:                          Multiple Clouds (Hybrid/Multicloud):
Services developed TOGETHER            Services developed INDEPENDENTLY,
→ integration mostly "just works"       often WITHOUT each other in mind
                                        → integration becomes a REAL challenge
```

## 2. Matching Workloads to Cloud Platforms BEFORE Integration

### 2.1 Understand Your Workload Needs FIRST

> **Before you make your selections of cloud platforms and services, make sure that you understand the NEEDS of the workloads you plan on deploying to your upcoming cloud ecosystem.**

### 2.2 Every Platform Has Different Strengths and Weaknesses

> **Each cloud platform has different strengths and weaknesses, and finding GOOD MATCHES for your workloads will MAXIMIZE your success.**

- **Practical implication:** the integration challenge is easier to manage well when the **underlying platform selections** were already **well-matched** to the workloads in the first place — good matching upfront reduces the integration burden downstream.

### 2.3 Assembling the Ecosystem — The Two-Part Challenge

> Once you understand your workloads' needs and have matched them to cloud platforms and services, you'll be ready to **assemble those clouds and services into your cloud ecosystem.**

- **The challenge then is TWO-FOLD:**
    1. **Integrate your cloud resources TOGETHER.**
    2. **Integrate your WORKLOADS with the cloud resources.**

```
Step 1: Understand workload needs
        │
        ▼
Step 2: Match workloads to appropriate cloud platforms/services
        │
        ▼
Step 3: Assemble clouds + services into a cloud ECOSYSTEM
        │
        ▼
Step 4: INTEGRATE — (a) cloud resources with EACH OTHER
                     (b) workloads WITH the cloud resources
```

## 3. The Ongoing, Iterative Nature of the Process

### 3.1 This Is Not a One-Time Exercise

> **As time goes by and you have NEW workloads to deploy to your cloud ecosystem, you should REPEAT the process of checking your workload against the resources of your cloud ecosystem.**

### 3.2 Don't Fear Change — Constant Reexamination Is Healthy

> **Don't be afraid to make changes, as CONSTANT REEXAMINATION of the efficacy of your computing resources will drive any changes required to keep your cloud ecosystem WELL-TUNED and serving your needs.**

### 3.3 The Payoff of Having Already Built a Multicloud Strategy

> **If you've gone the EXTRA MILE to develop a multicloud strategy and portfolio, you're likely to find you already have EXPERIENCE with a cloud provider that is IDEAL for that new workload.**

- **This directly connects back to the earlier multicloud discussion:** having pre-vetted, diversified cloud relationships pays off not just as a hedge against disruption, but as a **head start** whenever a **new workload** needs a home.

## 5. Integration at the Service Level

### 5.1 The Core Problem — Duplicate Services Across Clouds

> **A multicloud ecosystem will probably have services in MULTIPLE cloud environments that address the SAME resources** (in other words, **each cloud environment will have a storage service**).

### 5.2 The Organizational Decision Required

> **The organization responsible for managing the cloud ecosystem will need to EITHER:**
1. **Select SPECIFIC services for SPECIFIC uses**, OR
2. **Let applications use the services available in their NATIVE cloud environment**, OR
3. **Require ALL applications to use a service in ONE SPECIFIC environment.**

### 5.3 Worked Example — Storage Service Integration

- **The setup:** each cloud provider in your ecosystem offers a **storage service**; **many** cloud providers offer **multiple TIERS of storage.**
    - Example: use a cloud's **flash storage** for **fast processing**, while using **cold (much less expensive) storage** for data held **just for compliance reasons.**

#### The Performance Rule of Thumb

> **Each application will have GOOD performance if you ALWAYS use the NATIVE storage server available in the SAME cloud environment as your application.**

#### The Cross-Cloud Penalty

> **On the other hand, if an application uses storage in a DIFFERENT cloud environment, there will be HIGHER LATENCY for the storage access, and it's likely to SERIOUSLY DEGRADE the application's performance.**

#### The Trade-off When Data Integration Matters More Than Raw Speed

> **However, if you have ANOTHER application that is integrating data from BOTH applications** and the **performance of THAT process is a HIGH PRIORITY**, you may want to **consider designating ONLY ONE cloud's storage service** for use by **BOTH applications.**

- **The cost of this choice:** *"That will slow down AT LEAST ONE of those applications (the one in a different cloud from the storage), but it will OPTIMIZE your data integration application."*

```
Scenario A: Each app uses ITS OWN cloud's native storage
   App 1 (Cloud A) ──► Storage A   (FAST, low latency)
   App 2 (Cloud B) ──► Storage B   (FAST, low latency)
   → Great individual app performance, but data integration
     BETWEEN App 1 and App 2's data is harder/slower

Scenario B: BOTH apps forced to use ONE cloud's storage
   App 1 (Cloud A) ──► Storage A   (FAST — native)
   App 2 (Cloud B) ──► Storage A   (SLOW — cross-cloud latency!)
   → App 2 is slowed down, but the DATA INTEGRATION process
     (which needs both apps' data together) is now OPTIMIZED
```

### 5.4 Worked Example — Identity Management Integration

- **The scenario:** you have an **enterprise application** that serves **company employees** in the cloud.
- **The need:** access to the enterprise's **identity management system** to **authenticate those employees** — but that **Active Directory** or **LDAP (Lightweight Directory Access Protocol)** identity system **probably resides in the enterprise's corporate data center.**

#### If the Data Center Is Part of a Hybrid Cloud

> **If that data center is part of a HYBRID cloud, your application may be able to USE the on-premises identity management system DIRECTLY.**

#### Otherwise

> **Otherwise, you may need to find a COMPLEMENTARY identity management service** in one of the clouds in your ecosystem that can **run as a REMOTE VERSION** of the on-premises identity system.

```
Enterprise Application (in the cloud)
                │
                │ needs to authenticate employees
                ▼
Corporate Identity System (Active Directory / LDAP)
                │
   ┌────────────┴────────────┐
Data center is       Data center is NOT
part of a HYBRID     part of a hybrid cloud
cloud
   │                         │
   ▼                         ▼
Use on-premises       Find a COMPLEMENTARY identity
identity system        service running as a REMOTE
DIRECTLY               VERSION of the on-prem system
```

### 5.5 Worked Example — Disaster Recovery (DR) Service Integration

- **The goal:** when setting up DR services for your cloud ecosystem, you'll probably want **ALL of your replicated data stored in ONE PLACE.**

#### The Default Behavior Problem

> **If you use the DR services provided by EACH cloud of your ecosystem, their DEFAULT ACTION will be to STORE your data in THEIR OWN designated locations.**

#### Possible Fixes, in Order of Preference

1. **At the least**, you'll need to **CONFIGURE them, if possible, to store data in the SAME location.**
2. **Otherwise**, you may need to **designate ONE cloud's DR service** to **handle ALL application data**, **assuming that is possible.**
3. **If NEITHER of those scenarios is possible**, it may force you to **find an INDEPENDENT DR solution** or **build OTHER integration solutions.**

```
Each cloud's DR service DEFAULT behavior:
   Cloud A's DR ──► stores data at Cloud A's own location
   Cloud B's DR ──► stores data at Cloud B's own location
   (SCATTERED — not what you want!)

Fix attempt 1: Configure ALL DR services to store data
               in the SAME location (if possible)

Fix attempt 2: Designate ONE cloud's DR service to
               handle ALL application data (if possible)

Fix attempt 3 (last resort): Build an independent DR
               solution / custom integration
```

## 6. Integration at the Data Level

### 6.1 Why This Is the Most Common Integration Need

> **Data integration is PROBABLY the integration you're MOST LIKELY to require.**

- **The underlying business need:** *"Businesses need access to ALL their data in a consistent, easy, and integrated way, EVEN WHEN managed by SEPARATE applications."*
- *(Noted as discussed in greater depth in Chapter 14 of the source material.)*

### 6.2 Common Data Challenges

#### Challenge 1 — Data Silos

> **Data tied to different applications becomes ATTACHED to those applications. These SEGREGATED pockets of data are often called SILOS.**

#### Challenge 2 — The Work Required to Break Down Silos

> **Creating a CONSISTENT and COMPREHENSIVE view of data maintained in silos requires:**
1. **Access to EACH application's data.**
2. **Transformations** to make it **consistent.**
3. **A way of STORING the integrated data** so that **other applications can use it.**

- **A concrete labeling example (from context):** one data set may label states as **"St"** while others label them as **"States"** — necessitating a **common data glossary and definitions** across the environment to **achieve consistency.**

#### Challenge 3 — Applications Keep Running (and Changing) During Integration

> **Generally, you'll NOT be able to STOP applications while you access and transform their data**, and **applications will CONTINUE to change their data while you're accessing it.**

#### Challenge 4 — The Single Source of Truth Requirement

> **All critical data must have a SINGLE SOURCE OF TRUTH.** In other words, **integrating data must NOT obscure the PRIMARY location and ACCURACY of data.**

### 6.3 The Overall Difficulty

> **As you can see, integrating data across a broad set of applications and their databases can be a CHALLENGE.**

```
        Data Silos (App A's data, App B's data, App C's data...)
                    │
                    ▼
    Need: CONSISTENT, COMPREHENSIVE view across all silos
                    │
        ┌───────────┼───────────┐
        ▼           ▼           ▼
   Access each   Apply         Store integrated
   app's data    transforms    data for OTHER
                 (consistency)  apps to use
                    │
                    ▼
   CONSTRAINTS while doing this:
   • Can't stop the applications (they keep running)
   • Data keeps CHANGING while you access it
   • Must preserve a SINGLE SOURCE OF TRUTH
     (integration must not obscure the real, accurate data location)
```

## 7. Integration at the Application Level

### 7.1 Not a New Discipline — But a Harder Context

> **Application integration is a LONG-PRACTICED discipline with LOTS of technical experience available. Techniques are the SAME whether the context is a physical data center or a cloud environment.**

- **What's actually different here:** *"the difference here is the POTENTIAL NUMBER of environments that are involved."*
- **Concrete scenario:** if a company has created a **multicloud ecosystem**, applications that need to be integrated may be running:
    - **On premises.**
    - **In a hybrid-cloud context.**
    - **In multiple public clouds.**

### 7.2 Application Integration Often Extends/Modifies Business Processes

> **Many application integrations SEEK TO EXTEND or MODIFY business processes.**

#### Worked Example — Retail Credit Checking

- **Scenario:** a retail business **extends credit to its customers**, but the **initial implementation DIDN'T include a step** where the application would **check to verify the customer's credit rating** is in good shape **before extending the credit.**
- **The fix:** the application needs to be **INTEGRATED with a credit agency** to provide that **credit rating check.**
- **The consequence:** *"This step CHANGES the credit business process."*

#### Business Process Rules — Formal or Not

- **Applications that use BUSINESS PROCESS RULES** to guide the steps of a business process will **ADAPT those rules** as part of the integration.
- **BUT:** *"whether the application uses formal business process rules or NOT, the credit rating service will HAVE TO BE integrated with the retail application"* — the integration requirement exists **regardless** of how formally the business logic is structured.

### 7.3 The Organizational Reality of Multicloud Companies

> **Companies that embrace a multicloud ecosystem are likely to have made a SIGNIFICANT COMMITMENT to running their business in the cloud.**

- **What this typically means:** the company probably **manages MANY applications in the cloud**, likely organized in **INDEPENDENT computing environments**, to suit the needs of **specific independent business divisions.**
- **Example divisions:** **finance, HR, sales, and marketing** — probably ALL have **dedicated computing environments**, each with its **OWN needs for application integration.**
- **Hybrid patterns within divisions:** some divisions may run workloads in a **hybrid context** — e.g., **processing in the cloud**, but **data storage ON PREMISES** to meet **regulatory and auditing requirements.**

```
     Multicloud Company
   ┌───────┬───────┬───────┬─────────┐
   │Finance│  HR   │ Sales │Marketing│
   │(own   │(own   │(own   │(own     │
   │compute│compute│compute│compute  │
   │env.)  │env.)  │env.)  │env.)    │
   └───────┴───────┴───────┴─────────┘
   Each division: own application integration needs;
   some may be HYBRID (cloud processing + on-prem storage
   for regulatory/auditing compliance)
```

### 7.4 The Diversity-Driven Challenges

> **This DIVERSITY of environments and applications creates SOME CHALLENGES not found in single-environment computing contexts.**

#### Challenge Example 1 — Replacing an On-Premises App With Its SaaS Version

- **Scenario:** in setting up a hybrid context, a company may **replace its legacy on-premises application** with the **SaaS version of the SAME application** — but **the APIs may DIFFER** between the **on-premises and SaaS versions.**
- **Consequences:**
    1. **Previous integrations** involving the on-premises application **may NOT work** with the SaaS version.
    2. **Even if they DO work**, the integration may need to work **BETWEEN the SaaS application and OTHER on-premises applications** — requiring:
        - **Networking**, and
        - Perhaps **NEW governance controls**, since the **SaaS may be running with a DIFFERENT identity** than the on-premises version.

#### Challenge Example 2 — Same Application, Different Clouds

- **Scenario:** in a multicloud context, **independent clouds may run the SAME application.**
- **Why a business would set this up:**
    1. **Comparing performance** between clouds.
    2. **Developing portability controls** so the application can be **easily moved** between different clouds.
- **The complication:** if the application being moved **must integrate with OTHER applications** in **different cloud contexts**, but those **other applications HAVE DIFFERENT VERSIONS** in **other contexts**, **ADDITIONAL WORK will be required** to **ensure the integration works in ALL contexts.**

```
Same App, deployed in Cloud A AND Cloud B
                 │
                 ▼
Needs to integrate with "Other App"
                 │
   ┌─────────────┴─────────────┐
"Other App"            "Other App"
version in              version in
Cloud A                 Cloud B
   │                       │
   ▼                       ▼
Integration must WORK CORRECTLY
regardless of WHICH cloud context
the main app is currently running in
= EXTRA WORK to ensure consistency
across ALL contexts
```

## 8. The Role of APIs and Standards

### 8.1 Why APIs and Standards Matter Across ALL Three Integration Types

> **ALL three types of integration benefit from APIs and standards**, as they **often represent the POINTS OF INTEGRATION** between services, applications, and systems.

### 8.2 The Tension Between Innovation and Stability

> **Although the innovative and QUICKLY CHANGING world of the cloud means that APIs and standards often CHANGE with those new innovations**, vendors and developers **focus on creating CLEAR interfaces** and **try to PROTECT the APIs from change.**

### 8.3 Different Levels of API Stability Discipline

- **Cloud vendors:** take **particular care** in defining **STABLE APIs** for the services they provide.
- **Applications themselves:** tend to **define their OWN APIs**, **WITHOUT attempting to be consistent** with their competition.

```
Cloud Vendor APIs:                     Application-Level APIs:
Deliberately kept STABLE,               Defined independently by
carefully protected from                each application, with NO
unnecessary change                      attempt at cross-vendor
                                         consistency
```

### 8.4 What's Improved, and What Remains Hard

> **The evolution of APIs and well-defined computing interfaces has DEFINITELY made performing SOME types of integration EASIER**, but **DIFFICULT challenges STILL REMAIN**, **particularly when the systems being integrated have:**
- **Different data models.**
- **Different business flows.**
- **Different sophistication of APIs.**

## 9. Responsibility for Integrations — Who Does What

### 9.1 A Business Requirement, Implemented at Many Levels

> **The need to integrate is a business requirement**, but **integrations are IMPLEMENTED at DIFFERENT business and technical levels by DIFFERENT types of worker.**

### 9.2 Service-Level Integration — Cloud Engineers

- **Typically performed by:** **cloud engineers** who **understand the nature of cloud services.**
- **What they judge:**
    1. **Which services are the BEST** for **specific workloads.**
    2. Whether **independent services can be integrated together** to **create a NEW hybrid service.**

### 9.3 Data-Level Integration — A Multi-Role Process

**Step 1 — Domain Experts**
- **Data-level integrations require DOMAIN EXPERTS** to **understand the structure and meaning** of the data that must be integrated.
- **Worked example:** if an application being integrated is a **BANKING application**, the team performing the integration will need to **understand BANKING RULES.**

**Step 2 — Software Engineers**
- **Once the nature of the data is understood**, it often **falls to software engineers** to **implement the integration** with:
    - **Data connectors.**
    - **Data transforms.**
    - **Mapping tables.**
    - **Encryption of sensitive data.**
    - **Other piece-parts** of the data integration trade.

**Step 3 — Database Administrators**
- Database administrators will **create the schemas, tables, import functions**, and **other parts of organizing the integrated data** into a form that **applications can use.**

```
Data-Level Integration Pipeline:

Domain Experts                Software Engineers              Database Administrators
(understand the DATA'S         (IMPLEMENT the integration:      (ORGANIZE the integrated
 structure & meaning —          connectors, transforms,          data: schemas, tables,
 e.g., banking rules for a      mapping tables, encryption)      import functions)
 banking application)
        │                              │                               │
        └──────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────┘
                    Sequential collaboration across three distinct roles
```

### 9.4 Application-Level Integration — Software Engineers

> **Finally, application-level integration is a QUINTESSENTIALLY TECHNICAL activity**, calling for **software engineers** to look for **appropriate application APIs**, and perhaps **build connectors or adapters** when **suitable APIs are not found.**

#### But the NEED Still Comes From the Business

> **Granted, the need for application integration usually comes from BUSINESS requirements.**

- **Example:** the use of a **big data application** will **likely need cooperating VISUALIZATION applications** to help **interpret the results** of big data analysis.

#### The Multicloud Version-Consistency Wrinkle

> **Also, because multicloud environments may include DIFFERENT VERSIONS of the SAME application running in different contexts, integrating the different versions may be EASY, but MAY STILL REQUIRE transformations or other processing** to make the **different versions work together SEAMLESSLY.**

### 9.5 Summary — The Big Picture on Responsibility

> **As you can see, MANY of these integrations will be performed by SOFTWARE DEVELOPERS who will know the BEST TECHNICAL MEANS of solving those integration problems.**

## 10. Glossary of Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Cloud ecosystem** | The assembled collection of cloud platforms and services a business uses together to meet its computing needs. |
| **Service-level integration** | Connecting/coordinating cloud services (e.g., storage, identity management, DR) that exist independently across multiple cloud environments. |
| **Data-level integration** | Making business information stored across different applications/databases accessible in a consistent, standardized form. |
| **Application-level integration** | Connecting applications (often across different cloud contexts) to increase functionality, portability, and support data integration. |
| **Native storage server** | The storage service belonging to the same cloud environment as a given application, offering the lowest latency. |
| **Active Directory / LDAP** | Common enterprise identity management systems, typically residing in a corporate on-premises data center. |
| **Data silo** | A segregated pocket of data tied to and attached to a specific application. |
| **Single source of truth** | The principle that critical data must have one clear, authoritative, accurate primary location, not obscured by integration. |
| **Business process rules** | Formal rules guiding the steps of a business process, which may need adaptation when new integrations (e.g., credit checks) are added. |
| **Portability controls** | Mechanisms developed to allow an application to be easily moved between different cloud environments. |
| **Cloud engineer** | The role typically responsible for service-level integration decisions. |
| **Domain expert** | A role providing understanding of a dataset's structure and meaning (e.g., banking rules) as the first step in data-level integration. |
| **Connector / adapter** | Software built by engineers to bridge applications when suitable APIs aren't already available. |

## 11. Summary Tables

### 11.1 The Three Types of Integration

| Type | Core Challenge | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Service level | Same-purpose services exist in multiple clouds; must choose selection strategy | Storage, identity management, DR services |
| Data level | Data is siloed per application; needs consistent, integrated, single-source-of-truth access | Banking data, labeling inconsistencies (e.g., "St" vs "States") |
| Application level | Applications across environments need to work together, extend business processes | Credit-check integration, big data + visualization apps |

### 11.2 Storage Service Integration — Two Scenarios

| Scenario | Effect |
|---|---|
| Each app uses its own cloud's native storage | Best individual app performance; harder cross-app data integration |
| Both apps forced onto one cloud's storage | One app slowed by cross-cloud latency; data integration process optimized |

### 11.3 Identity Management Integration — Two Paths

| Condition | Solution |
|---|---|
| Data center is part of a hybrid cloud | Use the on-premises identity system directly |
| Data center is NOT part of a hybrid cloud | Find a complementary identity service running as a remote version |

### 11.4 DR Service Integration — Fallback Order

| Priority | Approach |
|---|---|
| 1st choice | Configure all cloud DR services to store data in the same location |
| 2nd choice | Designate one cloud's DR service to handle all application data |
| Last resort | Build an independent DR solution or custom integration |

### 11.5 Responsibility by Integration Type

| Integration Type | Primary Role(s) Responsible |
|---|---|
| Service level | Cloud engineers |
| Data level | Domain experts → Software engineers → Database administrators |
| Application level | Software engineers (finding/building APIs, connectors, adapters) |

## 12. Big-Picture Takeaways

1. **Integration only becomes a serious challenge once you move beyond a single cloud** — services within one platform are typically co-developed and interoperate naturally, but hybrid and multicloud configurations force you to bridge systems that were never designed with each other in mind.
2. **Getting the workload-to-platform match right BEFORE assembling your ecosystem reduces the integration burden you'll face later** — this is a proactive, upfront investment that pays dividends throughout the integration process.
3. **The three integration types (service, data, application) require progressively deeper business and technical understanding** — service-level integration is largely a cloud-engineering judgment call, data-level integration demands genuine domain expertise (e.g., banking rules) before any code gets written, and application-level integration often requires actually changing business processes, not just connecting systems.
4. **The storage integration trade-off (native performance vs. cross-cloud data integration) exemplifies a recurring theme**: you frequently can't optimize for every goal simultaneously, and choosing which application "takes the latency hit" is a genuine architectural decision, not an oversight to be avoided.
5. **Data silos and the single-source-of-truth requirement are in constant tension** — the very act of integrating data (accessing, transforming, storing a unified view) risks creating confusion about which copy is authoritative, unless deliberately guarded against.
6. **Multicloud's organizational reality — independent divisions (finance, HR, sales, marketing) each running their own computing environments — means integration challenges compound rather than simplify as a company's cloud footprint grows**, especially when SaaS migrations introduce API and identity differences that break previously-working integrations.
7. **APIs and standards are the connective tissue of all three integration types, but stability discipline varies sharply by source** — cloud vendors carefully protect their service APIs from change, while individual applications define their own APIs with little regard for cross-vendor consistency, meaning integration difficulty often traces directly back to whose API you're depending on.
8. **Integration responsibility flows through a chain of distinct expertise, not a single team** — from cloud engineers making service-selection judgment calls, through domain experts who understand what the data actually MEANS, to software engineers who implement the technical connectors, to database administrators who organize the final integrated result — reinforcing that successful integration is fundamentally a cross-functional, not purely technical, endeavor.
